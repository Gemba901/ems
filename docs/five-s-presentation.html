Before you begin

  1. Open the staging company address and sign in as an Admin with an employee profile. Confirm the company has the 5S Workplace module enabled in its module settings.
  2. Open 5S Workplace. In Settings → Sample data, click Load sample data if it is not already loaded. Use sample records for the presentation.
  3. Confirm two sample areas appear: [Sample] Press Shop Line 1 and [Sample] Stores Receiving Bay. The press shop has a submitted audit, actions, a red tag, an approved standard and a daily routine. Stores is unassessed.
  4. Have a second account ready: an employee assigned an action, and a different HOD or authorised verifier. An action owner cannot verify their own work. Use separate browser profiles to keep logins separate.
  5. Open Overview, the sample submitted audit, an open action, the sample red tag and Standards & Routines in tabs. Check them before presenting. If staging is still deploying, use the prepared local environment and explain that it is the demonstration environment.
  6. Do not remove sample data during the presentation. Removing it also removes records created inside the sample areas.

1. Introduce the purpose · 30 seconds

“This module supports Key 1 of the 20 Keys: cleaning and organising to make work easy. We define workplace areas, assess their condition, assign improvements, and sustain the changes through standards and routines.”

Use one story throughout: a press shop with misplaced tools, an unidentified spare clamp and an end-of-shift cleaning check.

2. Show the overview · 1 minute

  1. Click Overview in the sidebar.
  2. Point out areas assessed, target met, open and overdue actions, awaiting verification, pending red tags and audits overdue.
  3. In Latest 5S level by area, compare the sample press shop with the unassessed stores area. Unassessed does not mean zero.
  4. Show the per-S abnormality chart and submitted audit history. Explain that drafts do not contribute results.
“This tells a supervisor where to focus: which areas have been assessed, which missed their target, and which follow-up work needs attention.”

3. Define the workplace area · 1 minute

  1. Click Workplace Areas and open the sample press shop.
  2. Show the department, owner, audit boundary, target level and audit schedule.
  3. If demonstrating creation, click New area. Use “Presentation — Tool Store”, select a department and owner, set a target of 3.0, and define a precise boundary such as “Tool rack A and adjacent workbench”. Otherwise, explain the fields on the existing area.
“A clear boundary makes repeat audits comparable. The owner is responsible for maintaining the condition of this area.”

4. Run an audit · 3 minutes

  1. Click 5S Audits → Start an audit, or start one from an area. Select the sample press shop, today’s date, auditor, auditee and agreed boundary.
  2. Create the draft. Show the five practices: Sort, Set in Order, Shine, Standardize and Sustain. There are 20 checkpoints, four per practice.
  3. Answer a few checkpoints. Explain that you count problem locations, rather than individual items: several unwanted items on one rack count as one problem location.
  4. Show that choices run from 0 to 5+. A blank means unanswered; zero is an explicit answer. Add a remark identifying the location. Photos are optional.
  5. Click Save and finish later, then reopen the draft to demonstrate saved progress. Allow each save to finish before leaving.
  6. For a quick complete example, enter 1 at checkpoint 1 and 1 at checkpoint 5; enter 0 for the other 18. The result is 2 points, level 3.4, with two actions on submission.
  7. Click Review and submit. Check the result and action count, then Submit and lock. Submitted audits cannot be edited.
“Lower abnormality points are better; a higher 5S level is better. Submission locks the assessment and creates one action for each checkpoint where a problem was found.”

Time-saving option: Show a partial draft, then switch to the prepared submitted sample audit to explain the completed result.

Explain the scoring in 20 seconds

MeasureMeaning
Each checkpoint0–5 points; 5+ problem locations count as 5
Each practiceFour checkpoints, 0–20 points
Total0–100 points; fewer is better
Overall level3.5 − ceiling(total ÷ 4) × 0.1; from 1.0 to 3.5

Examples: 0 points → 3.5; 1–4 → 3.4; 5–8 → 3.3; 17–20 → 3.0; 27 → 2.8. The formula applies once to the overall total. Levels are not percentages.

5. Follow an improvement action · 2 minutes

  1. Click Improvement Actions and open an audit-generated action. Show its source audit, checkpoint, finding, owner and history.
  2. As the coordinator, assign a corrective action, an employee owner and a due date. Example: “Mark the tool positions and return each tool to the shadow board.”
  3. As the action owner, describe the completed work, add evidence if available, and submit it for verification. Status becomes Awaiting verification.
  4. Switch to the different authorised verifier. Verify the work, or return it for rework with a reason. Show the history.
“Completion is checked by someone other than the action owner. Closing an action preserves the original audit score; the next audit measures the improvement.”

If the second account is unavailable, show the action details and explain the remaining steps. An action without a due date is shown as “Due date not set” and is not counted as overdue.

6. Handle an unwanted item · 1–2 minutes

  1. Click Red-Tag Register and open the sample unidentified spare die clamp.
  2. Show item, location, quantity, reason and owner. Explain how a worker can raise a tag.
  3. As the responsible reviewer, record a decision. For a simple demonstration, choose an appropriate non-disposal decision from the available options.
  4. Record the final disposition after the decision has been carried out. Only then does the tag become resolved.
“A red tag is a controlled decision about an item. It stays pending until the decision and final disposition have both been recorded.”

Disposal requires an approval reference. The register does not delete asset records.

7. Sustain the improvement · 2 minutes

  1. Click Standards & Routines and open “SAMPLE: Die rack layout and labelling”. Show the current approved revision.
  2. Explain draft → approval by the area HOD or 5S administrator. To change it, start a new revision; approving that revision supersedes the old one while preserving history.
  3. Open “SAMPLE: End-of-shift clean and return tools”. Show the location, responsible person, frequency and scheduled checks.
  4. Record an available scheduled check as done, done with an exception, or not done. Describe the exception if required. A scheduled occurrence can only be recorded once.
“The standard defines the agreed condition. The routine checks that people maintain it between audits.”

Routines support daily, weekly and monthly checks. Unrecorded past checks show as missed. Routine checks do not alter audit scores or automatically create actions.

8. Show control and close · 1 minute

  1. Open Settings. Show module responsibilities and the change log. Settings is limited to 5S administrators.
  2. Explain that management viewers can review company-wide results, while other users’ access follows their department, responsibilities and record ownership.
  3. Open How to Use to show the built-in reference for checkpoints, counting, scoring and roles.
  4. Return to Overview and show that the submitted assessment and follow-up work are visible.
“The workflow is: define the area, assess it, fix the findings, verify the work, and sustain the result. The next audit tells us whether the workplace actually improved.”

Likely questions

QuestionAnswer
Why isn’t this a percentage?The checksheet converts abnormality points into a 1.0–3.5 level.
Can I submit an incomplete audit?No. All 20 checkpoints need explicit answers.
Can I change a submitted audit?No. Submission locks the record and result.
Does closing actions improve the score?The original result stays fixed. Improvement is measured at the next audit.
Can I verify my own action?No. Verification requires a different authorised person.
Is a decided red tag resolved?No. Final disposition must also be recorded.
Can we use it for Model Office scoring?This release implements the production/shopfloor checksheet.
Can we remove training records?Yes. An administrator can remove records in the sample areas through Settings.

If you only have five minutes

  1. Overview and area ownership — 45 seconds.
  2. Prepared submitted audit: 20 checkpoints, points, level and lock — 90 seconds.
  3. One action: owner, due date, completion, independent verification — 60 seconds.
  4. One red tag: decision followed by disposition — 45 seconds.
  5. Approved standard and routine schedule — 45 seconds.
  6. Close with the next audit measuring improvement — 15 seconds.
